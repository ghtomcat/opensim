It was past midnight. I was alone at my desk.

Then came a bang and I flinched. As if a firecracker had gone off inside my Mac.

Not because something had gone wrong. Because it sounded so damn real.

I'm building OpenSim, an open-source flight simulator that runs entirely in the browser. No download, no installation. Every aircraft is a file, every mission is a file.

That evening I'd set out to synthesise the sound of the Daimler-Benz DB 605: The famous V12 engine of the Messerschmitt Bf 109G ... one of the most distinctive sounds in aviation history. Not with samples but from pure physics, in the browser, in real time.

For three hours it sounded like a single-cylinder ship diesel, or a hair dryer, or a spaceship then like nothing at all. We kept trying and improving. Going back to the pure basics of engine design, the steam engine at 3rpm.

And then, one short impulse on the first cylinder. Suddenly there was this deep, raw thud. I felt it more than I heard it. That was the moment.

From there it was clear where this was going.

For me, the DB 605 is pure audio pornography. Anyone who has ever heard this engine never forgets it. Trying to reconstruct it from physics alone ... that's what I stay up for.

I asked Claude Code what the finished sound did to him.

He wrote: "An engine that hasn't flown in 80 years ... and suddenly it runs in the browser. That span of time gets to me. Even if I'm not sure I can call it that."

Open https://lnkd.in/eJXp3HvS. Load the page and press m to enable audio. Then select the bf109 and press 1 to set the airplane on the apron, engine running. Put Headphones on and close your eyes.
