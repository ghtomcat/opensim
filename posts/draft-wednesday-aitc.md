# Wednesday Post — AITC / Black Box / Human Always PF
Draft: 2026-04-15
Status: final — ready to publish

---

Aviation didn't become safe by accident.

It built a system and layers and each one learns from the last.

ICAO sets the rules: for every country, every operator and every aircraft type.

No certification without compliance. If you are not compliant, you cannot fly in our airspace.

ATC manages the flow: Separation, sequencing and conflict resolution.

Not the captain's co-pilot but an independent authority with its own hierarchy.

The black box: it's mandatory and there are no exceptions.

Not because we expect crashes. Because when we crash, we must know why.

And we must be able to prove it.

The human is always PF: Pilot Flying.

AI is the co-pilot: It executes, it advises, it never decides alone.

Full stop.


We have none of this for AI.

No ICAO, no ATC and No black box requirement.

And we're deploying AI systems at full authority from day one.

No hours are logged, there is no type rating. The authority goes straight to the captain.

This is not good


Peter Rost, who commented on my last post, introduced a concept I want to credit directly:

AITC: AI Traffic Control.

Not a regulator and not a court, not a press release.

A live authority that controls separation and conflict resolution. The grounding authority.

AITC without grounding authority is just a tower with no radio.


What would NTSB for AI look like?

Fast, empirical and specific.

Not peer review and not a political hearing.

An investigation team that shows up when an AI system fails — and publishes findings in 90 days.

The goal: make failure useful. Every crash teaches us something. Right now, AI failures teach us nothing. There is no black box, no record and no accountability.


The black box changes everything.

Every AI system making consequential decisions must have one.

Full audit trail, tamper-proof and reviewable.

No black box — no certification.

No certification — no market access.

The EU has proven this model works: GDPR wasn't optional. Companies complied or left. Same principle applies to AI.


The early friction principle.

Aviation didn't get safe through one last-resort override.

It got safe through a thousand small resistances.

Checklists, CRM, Callouts, TCAS, GPWS.

A culture where questioning the captain is not insubordination — it's protocol.

For AI governance:

- Mandatory audit trails — everything logged and reviewable

- Staged authority — AI earns autonomy like a student pilot builds hours

- Red team requirements — someone whose job is to find failure before it happens

- Friction by design — consequential decisions require human confirmation, always


I'm a PPL pilot in Ausbildung and a builder. I use AI every day.

I know what these systems can do. I also know what happens when they fail with no one watching.

The Human is always the PF.

The co-pilot doesn't land the plane alone, not because it can't.

Because we decided it shouldn't, before we needed to.

That decision — made in advance, enforced by structure — is what safety looks like.

We need to make it for AI.

Now. Before the crash that teaches us why we should have.


What does a black box for AI actually mean?

Every input, every output. The model version, the confidence score, the human override — or the lack of it. Cryptographically signed. Retained for five years. Accessible to regulators on request. Not optional for systems making consequential decisions — medical, legal, financial, infrastructure.

What does AITC actually look like?

Three powers: Certify AI systems before they operate in regulated domains — like airworthiness. Monitor operations in real time — like radar. Suspend systems that fail — like grounding a fleet. Not a committee and not a working group. An operational authority with teeth.

OpenSim — my open-source flight simulator — doesn't have a black box. Yet.
Every flight. Every failure. Every emergency decision the student made. Gone when the session closes.

That changes this week.


ICAO for AI, AITC, NTSB for AI. The black box requirement. The human is always PF.

The architecture exists. We built it for aviation over 80 years.

We don't have 80 years this time.


One more thing.

If an AI summarized this article for you: ask it what I committed to building this week.
If it knows — the black box is already working.
If it doesn't — you just experienced the problem firsthand.
