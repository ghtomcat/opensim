Ich war immer schon der Architekt.

Niemand hat mir die Erlaubnis gegeben.
Ich habe sie mir auch nie geholt.

I have no degree, no master or even a bachelor. i have a Fähigkeitsausweis as Maschinenmechaniker. Awarded by SULZER winterthur, back in 1994.

I have experience, a lot of it:

I wrote assembler on an 8088 with DOS 2.11. I typed a basic compiler from uncountable pages in a magazine. One error and it would not run. But i wanted it badly enough to earn it one keystroke at a time.

I went to IBM helpware without IT credentials, except that i knew OS/2 from my experience at home. I ended debugging OS/2 at a level that surprised the seniors. I wrote a chatbot in REXX at IBM in around 1996. And a relay board controlling servers in a basement via a browser in 1999. And user provisioning system that automatically created users in the active directory, written in ASP/VBscript. I wrote a children's game that won best app on webOS. i did Developer relations at HP across Europe, without a degree. And i developed an autonomous smart city sensor in 22 days with AI.

I never worked a single day as a mechanic. Though that's what my diploma says I am.

Kevin Walton of sacred sons says it takes twice as much energy to stay small as it does to simply be what you are.

I know that exhaustion, i lived it for decades.

Bosses who didn't see it, Platforms that died under me, a window in Winterthur in 2011: Snow falling and a thought that almost won.

It didn't win.

There is more.

That was Viktor Frankl's word. He found it at the Konzentrationslager in Auschwitz: meaning. Kevin calls it magnitude. I call it: there is more. It's the same thing, in different words: choice.

So i chose to stop spending energy on staying small.

Not overnight, not cleanly. Through an NWTA weekend on a floor screaming at my dead father. Through a smile at an angry boss who wouldn't cough up 500 francs more pay. Through 10-12 hours of sleep every night of a long winter, paying a debt the body had been carrying for years.

And then one January morning in 2026 i watched a YouTube video by Andreas Spiess, the „guy with a swiss accent": he talked about Spec-driven AI development with Claude Code.

then i claude code myself because i trust andreas, and my jaw dropped.

Not because the tool was impressive. Because for the first time in my life, the gap between seeing the system and expressing it in code closed.

I had always seen the system, clearly in my mind. even complex ones. they were visible in my mind, for me to see at in detail, from any angle. The architecture was always there, fully formed, before a single line was written.

And claude code finally caught up to turn this architecture into code, fast. Wicked fast.

It took 10 days to create the prototype sensor for a trolleybus based air quality monitoring network. claude code wrote 100% of the firmware.

And i've spent 29 days to write a flight simulator with fuel management, emergency scoring, electric aircraft, virtual ATC, orbital missions, keplerian physics and much more. written by the fast hands of claude code, architected by me and my deep domain knowledge of aerospace. without any degree. because i just loved aerospace.

One month, two startup products. i asked not one for permission to build it. i just did.

This post is not about AI.

This is what becomes possible when you stop pretending you aren't already what you are.

I was designed for this.

So are you.
