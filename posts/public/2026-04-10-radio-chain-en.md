---

Around 2030, I plan to launch on a SpaceX Falcon 9 with people I trust completely:

My Pilot writes poetry and grows plants and builds with wood. She's also, I suspect, a natural pilot. My Mission Specialist comes from Nepal. And Mission Specialist 2 is still TBD — but she'll be a young African woman, probably a student. Because that's what this mission is about.

Access for people the system wasn't built for.

---

We're going to rehearse this mission for years before we fly it: Same callouts, same comm rhythm, same crew. So when we're strapped in at LC-39A, none of it will be new.

Last week I added something to the simulator that changes what that rehearsal sounds like.

---

My pilot can now record her lines on my Zoom H4n Pro Audio recorder: Her actual voice, in the sim. "Drogues out. Descent nominal." "Brace for impact."

My Mission Specialist records his next time he's in Switzerland. MS2 records hers wherever she is.

My lines are already in there.

---

But here's what makes it more than recordings:

Every voice passes through the correct radio equipment for its era. A filter chain built in Web Audio — the same technology that runs in any browser — that models the physical properties of the comm hardware. The Inspiration5 crew will sound like they're transmitting from inside a Dragon capsule. Because they will be.

---

There's a moment in the Wolfskopf mission — a Bf 109 in the Arctic, January 1942, DB 601 at full throttle — where the engine is already synthesised sample by sample via AudioWorklet. The engine IS the acoustic environment.

So when ATC comes through the radio in that cockpit, I tap the live engine output and mix it underneath the received voice, just slightly. The pilot hears the transmission but the DB 601 is still there.

Because it was never not there.

---

The same system works for historical missions: NASA audio is public domain. The real Gene Kranz, the real Charlie Duke — cut the clip, drop it in the mission folder, tell the sim what era it came from. Apollo 11 heard through the actual S-band character of a signal that had just crossed 380,000 kilometres.

Nothing like this exists in any simulator.
