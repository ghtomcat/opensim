# Code of Conduct

We don't shit on each other. Not "this is the enemy and these are the good guys." Every pilot on every side gets the same respect. That's the whole thing.

---

OpenSim welcomes contributors from anywhere in the world.  
The bar for contribution is accuracy and respect — not nationality, background, or which side your country was on.

---

## The standard

**Historical accuracy is the bar.**

If you know a particular aircraft, engine, battle, or pilot better than anyone — bring that knowledge. A Japanese developer who knows the Mitsubishi A6M better than any Western source, a Finnish developer who knows the Brewster Buffalo's role in the Winter War, a British developer who knows the Spitfire's Merlin engine from working on one — all of that is exactly what OpenSim needs.

Accuracy is how we honor the people who were there.

---

## What is welcome

- New aircraft, modeled correctly
- New missions, set on the right day and place, with real names where known
- Engine sounds calibrated from real recordings
- Corrections to existing work — if something is wrong, say so and fix it
- Translations and localization
- Documentation in any language

---

## What is not welcome

- Glorification of any side, ideology, or atrocity
- Caricature — of any nationality, any pilot, any aircraft
- Fictional missions presented as historical
- Sound, physics, or performance data that is invented rather than researched

If you are not sure whether something is accurate, say so. "I believe this is correct but could not verify" is an honest contribution. Presenting guesses as facts is not.

---

## How we treat each other

The same standard that applies to history applies to people.

Every contributor is taken seriously. Disagreements about accuracy are resolved with sources, not volume. If someone knows more than you about a particular aircraft or battle, that is an asset, not a threat.

We do not argue about which side was right. That is not what OpenSim is for.

Who you are outside the codebase is your business. Race, nationality, religion, gender, sexuality — none of it determines whether your contribution is welcome. Your knowledge and your accuracy do.

A trans developer who knows the Mitsubishi A6M. A gay developer who knows Apollo guidance systems. A developer from any country that was on any side of any war. All welcome. No exceptions.

Bullying, harassment, or exclusion on any of these grounds will be treated the same as a historically dishonest contribution: it does not belong here, and it will not be tolerated. The maintainer's judgment on this is also final.

---

## Enforcement

OpenSim operates as a benevolent dictatorship. Markus Leutwyler is the maintainer and has final say on everything that enters the codebase — no exceptions, no appeals process, no committee.

This is not arbitrary. It is the same model Linus Torvalds uses for the Linux kernel. One person with clear standards is a better filter than a committee with none.

If a contribution does not meet the standard — historically inaccurate, disrespectful, or in violation of the manifest — it does not merge. No negotiation required. The maintainer's judgment is final.

If you believe something in the project violates these principles, open an issue. Describe the problem clearly. It will be taken seriously.

If your contribution was rejected and you disagree, you are welcome to fork the project. That is what open source allows. But the standard here does not move.

---

*OpenSim is built on the belief that the people who flew these aircraft — on every side — deserve to be remembered accurately. That is the whole job.*
