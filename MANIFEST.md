# OpenSim Manifest

OpenSim is a flight simulation built on one principle: **realism as respect**.

Every aircraft modeled here carried a human being. Every engine sound is calibrated from real recordings. Every mission is set on the right day, over the right place, with the right names.

This is not a game. It is not entertainment in the casual sense. It is a place where history is taken seriously — because the people who made it deserve that.

---

## Who this is for

The warbird buff who has stood next to a running R-2800 and knows immediately whether it sounds right.

The family of a pilot who wants to understand what he flew, how it felt, what the cockpit looked like at fifteen thousand feet.

The student who has never heard of Big Ed Beeler or VF-17 and leaves knowing something real.

The developer anywhere in the world who knows a particular aircraft or battle better than anyone and wants to contribute that knowledge accurately.

---

## What we honor

Every pilot who flew. Every mechanic who kept the aircraft running. Every mission that happened.

We do not take sides in history. A nineteen-year-old climbing into a Zero over the Pacific deserves the same accuracy and respect as the one climbing into a Corsair. They were both someone's son. They both showed up. Many did not come home.

OpenSim honors duty. Not victory. Not ideology. Duty.

---

## What this is built on

- Open source. Anyone can contribute, inspect, improve.
- Historical accuracy as the non-negotiable standard.
- Physics that match reality, not what feels good to fly.
- Sound calibrated from real engines, real recordings.
- Names and dates that are correct.

---

## What this is not

OpenSim is not a platform for glorification, caricature, or propaganda.  
No side is portrayed as less than human.  
No aircraft is a cartoon.  
No pilot is a villain.

If you want to add something, the question is always: *does this honor the people who were actually there?*

If yes, you are welcome here.
